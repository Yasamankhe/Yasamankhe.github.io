Rover Lighting Control System

Designed, assembled, programmed, and validated a 4-layer STM32 and CAN based embedded controller for a rover electrical system. The board integrates power conversion, constant-current LED control, protection, communication, and embedded firmware in a single subsystem.

Project Progression

From PCB design and layout through assembly, hardware bring-up, and final integration onto the rover.

01

System Overview

The lighting controller was developed as an embedded subsystem for a rover electrical architecture. The board receives power from the rover battery system, generates regulated voltage rails, communicates over CAN, and controls the lighting output through an STM32 microcontroller.

The project covered the complete hardware lifecycle from circuit design and PCB layout through fabrication, hand assembly, firmware development, bench testing, and rover integration.

Power Input

Designed around the rover's 21 to 29.4 V battery range with regulated internal power rails.

STM32 Control

Embedded control handles commands, PWM behavior, communication, and lighting operation.

CAN Interface

CAN communication connects the controller to the wider rover electrical network.

Constant-Current LED Drive

Dedicated LED driving circuitry provides controlled current and PWM adjustable illumination.

Protection

Power and communication interfaces include protection against electrical transients.

Debug & Programming

SWD and communication interfaces support programming, hardware bring-up, and system debugging.

03

PCB Design

The controller was implemented as a four-layer PCB. Layout decisions focused on power distribution, grounding, switching regulator placement, communication routing, microcontroller support circuitry, and compact integration of the LED driving hardware.

Layer Stack
Four-layer construction with a dedicated ground reference and internal power distribution.
Power Layout
Switching regulator components were grouped around the converter to keep the high-current switching loop compact.
Control
STM32 control, programming, and communication circuitry were organized separately from the switching power section.
Interfaces
CAN, USB, UART, and SWD connections support communication, debugging, and firmware development.
04

Assembly & Bring-Up

Five copies of the controller were assembled and hand soldered. Bring-up began with staged power verification before programming the STM32 and testing the communication and lighting interfaces.

Initial Inspection
Boards were visually inspected for assembly faults, solder bridges, polarity, and connector orientation.
Power-Up
Voltage rails were verified before progressing to microcontroller programming and functional testing.
Firmware
STM32 firmware was loaded through the programming interface and used to exercise the lighting outputs.
Communication
USB-CAN testing was used to send commands, observe feedback, and verify system behavior.
05

Rover Integration

After bench validation, the lighting subsystem was installed on the rover and tested as part of the complete electrical system.

PWM control allowed the illumination level and behavior to be adjusted dynamically. The lighting system supported rover tasks involving Morse code signaling and changes in illumination as the rover approached obstacles during puzzle-solving activities.

From standalone board to rover subsystem

This stage verified that the controller could operate as part of the complete rover rather than only as an isolated bench prototype.
