Rover Lighting Control System

Designed, assembled, programmed, and validated a 4-layer STM32 and CAN-based embedded controller for a rover electrical system. The board integrates power conversion, constant-current LED control, protection, communication, and embedded firmware in a single subsystem.

4-layer PCB
21 to 29.4 V input
5 boards assembled
STM32 control
CAN communication
01

System Overview

The lighting controller was developed as an embedded subsystem for a rover electrical architecture. The board receives power from the rover battery system, generates regulated voltage rails, communicates over CAN, and controls the lighting output through an STM32 microcontroller.

The completed design was fabricated, hand assembled, brought up on the bench, programmed, validated through USB-CAN communication, and integrated onto the rover.

Power Input

Designed for a 21 to 29.4 V rover supply with conversion to the required internal voltage rails.

STM32 Control

Embedded microcontroller handles commands, lighting control, PWM behavior, and communication.

CAN Interface

CAN connectivity allows the lighting controller to operate as part of the rover electrical network.

LED Driver

Constant-current LED driving and PWM control provide controlled illumination and task-specific behavior.

Protection

Input and communication interfaces include protection against electrical transients and interface faults.

Debug & Programming

SWD and communication interfaces support firmware programming, hardware bring-up, and debugging.

03

PCB Design

The controller was implemented as a four-layer PCB with dedicated power distribution, a continuous ground reference, and routing organized around the switching regulator, microcontroller, CAN interface, and LED driver sections.

04

Assembly & Bring-Up

Five boards were assembled and hand soldered. Bring-up included power rail verification, microcontroller programming, communication testing, and functional testing of the lighting output.

05

Rover Integration

The completed lighting subsystem was installed and tested on the rover. PWM control allowed the lighting behavior to be adjusted dynamically and supported tasks involving Morse code signaling and lighting changes as the rover approached obstacles during puzzle-solving activities.
