02

Electromagnetic Solenoid Design

The solenoid had to be treated as an electromagnetic and mechanical system together. Magnetic materials, coil winding, the 12 V supply, current draw, actuator gap, plunger travel, and linkage geometry all affected whether the finger could press a key reliably.

The air gap was especially important because air has much lower permeability than the steel magnetic path. Even a relatively small gap can contribute strongly to the magnetic reluctance of the system. Reducing unnecessary separation between magnetic components therefore became one reason for repeatedly iterating the finger geometry and actuator placement.

Housing
A mild-steel housing was used so the housing could form part of the low-reluctance magnetic flux path.
Plunger
The mild-steel plunger formed the moving magnetic component and translated electromagnetic force into mechanical motion.
Guide Rod
304 stainless steel was used for mechanical guidance rather than as a major component of the magnetic flux path.
Coil
30 AWG insulated magnet wire was wound around the bobbin, with resistance measured throughout the winding process.
Electrical Range
The actuator was designed around a 12 V supply. A 20 Ω coil draws approximately 0.6 A, while a 15 Ω coil draws approximately 0.8 A. Coil resistance therefore directly constrained current draw and heating.
Mechanical Coupling
Linkage geometry and actuator placement were adjusted together to improve force transfer, alignment, and actuator spacing.
Electrical and mechanical design were coupled

Increasing current was not the only way to improve actuation. Mechanical geometry, air gap, magnetic materials, and the way force was transferred through the linkage all affected final key-press performance.

Machining & Fabrication

I was one of two members of the course team who were certified and authorized to machine metallic components in the machine shop. This made actuator fabrication one of my main responsibilities during the build.

I used the lathe and milling machine to fabricate and finish the metallic solenoid components, including the housings and plungers. The finished parts then had to mate with the printed bobbin, guide hardware, linkage, mounting hardware, and hand base.

Guide Rod

Fabrication Loop
Machining was followed by fit checks, mechanical assembly, coil installation, actuator testing, and additional revisions where necessary.
04

Finger & Linkage Iteration

The electromagnetic force generated by the solenoid still had to be transferred through the finger mechanism to the piano key. I therefore treated the mechanical linkage as part of the actuator system rather than as an independent component.

Multiple finger and joint revisions were printed with changes in linkage length, mounting position, and geometry. Some dimensions were changed in approximately 1 mm increments so I could compare the physical behavior of closely related designs.

Link Length
Different linkage lengths changed both the resting actuator position and the resulting key-contact geometry.
Mounting Position
Mounting points were moved between revisions to improve actuator alignment and mechanical motion.
Actuator Gap
Linkage geometry was also used to reduce unnecessary spacing in the magnetic actuator path.
Physical Testing
FDM printing made it possible to test several closely related geometries quickly on the real piano mechanism.
05

Wiring, Soldering & System Integration

Once the individual actuators worked, the harder problem became integrating them into a moving system. The carriage contained mechanical joints, solenoids, wiring, drive electronics, structural parts, and rail interfaces in a limited amount of space.

I worked on actuator wire routing, harness organization, soldering, and mechanical integration. Wiring had to remain clear of the finger joints and moving hardware while still allowing the carriage to travel along the keyboard.

Integration was iterative. Whenever the geometry or actuator position changed, I often had to re-check wire routing, soldered connections, clearances, and mechanical movement before testing the system again.

Harness Routing
Actuator wiring was organized so the bundle could move with the carriage without interfering with the fingers or rail mechanism.
Soldering
Coil leads and actuator connections had to remain electrically reliable while being integrated into a mechanically moving system.
Mechanical Clearance
Wiring, solenoids, fasteners, finger linkages, and structural parts were checked together for physical interference.
Final Integration
Actuator, finger, harness, carriage, electronics, and positioning behavior were tested together as one electromechanical system.
Integration was a major part of the project

A working solenoid or finger by itself was not enough. The actuator, linkage, wiring, carriage, electronics, and positioning system all had to work together without interfering with one another.

06

Key Positioning Model

I developed a Python positioning model using keyboard geometry, gear pitch, tooth count, and a calibratable hardware offset to calculate carriage positions across all 54 piano keys.

The script converts the physical position of each key into the motor-coordinate units used by the carriage. This provided a systematic way to generate the complete key position table rather than calibrating each location independently.

Python
Piano Key Position Mapping
key_positioning.py 
import math

PITCH = 2.0
TEETH = 39

MM_REV = TEETH * PITCH

MILLIRAD_REV = 2.0 * math.pi * 1000.0

TICKS_PER_MM = MILLIRAD_REV / MM_REV

OFFSET_TICKS = 0

NATURAL = 22.25
OCTAVE = 7 * NATURAL

CENTER = {
    'C': 0.5,
    'C#': 0.95,
    'D': 1.5,
    'D#': 2.05,
    'E': 2.5,
    'F': 3.5,
    'F#': 3.93,
    'G': 4.5,
    'G#': 5.0,
    'A': 5.5,
    'A#': 6.07,
    'B': 6.5
}

NOTES = [
    'C', 'C#', 'D', 'D#',
    'E', 'F', 'F#', 'G',
    'G#', 'A', 'A#', 'B'
]

NUM_KEYS = 54

keys = []

for i in range(NUM_KEYS):

    octave_index = i // 12

    note = NOTES[i % 12]

    mm = (
        octave_index * OCTAVE
        + CENTER[note] * NATURAL
    )

    keys.append(mm)


for i, mm in enumerate(keys):

    ticks = round(
        mm * TICKS_PER_MM
        + OFFSET_TICKS
    )

    comma = "," if i < NUM_KEYS - 1 else ""

    print(
        f"    {ticks:<8}{comma} [{i}]"
    )
07

Complete System Integration

The final system combined the custom electromagnetic actuators, printed finger joints, carriage structure, wiring, soldered actuator connections, drive electronics, linear motion system, and key-position model into one working robotic piano platform.

The carriage was not restricted to a single octave. The positioning system allowed the hand assembly to travel across the keyboard so the mechanism could reach all 54 mapped keys.
