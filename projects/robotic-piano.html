Electromagnetic Solenoid Design

The solenoid had to be treated as an electromagnetic and mechanical system together. The magnetic materials, coil winding, 12 V supply, current draw, air gap, plunger travel, and linkage geometry all affected whether the finger could reliably press a key.

The air gap was especially important because air has much lower permeability than the steel magnetic path. Even a small gap can contribute strongly to magnetic reluctance, so reducing unnecessary separation was one of the reasons for repeatedly iterating the finger geometry.

Housing
Mild steel was used so the housing could form part of the magnetic flux path.
Plunger
Mild-steel plunger formed the moving part of the magnetic circuit.
Guide Rod
304 stainless steel was used for mechanical guidance rather than as a major magnetic-path component.
Coil
30 AWG insulated magnet wire was wound around the bobbin, with resistance checked during winding.
Electrical Design
The actuator operated from a 12 V supply. Coil resistance was monitored because it directly determined current draw and heating.
Mechanical Coupling
Linkage geometry and actuator placement were adjusted together to improve force transfer and reduce unnecessary magnetic separation.
Electrical and mechanical design were coupled

Increasing current was not the only way to improve actuation. Mechanical geometry, air gap, magnetic materials, and force-transfer geometry all affected the final key-press performance.

04

Finger & Linkage Iteration

The solenoid force still had to be transferred through the finger mechanism to the piano key. I therefore treated the linkage geometry as part of the actuator design.

Multiple revisions were printed with changes in length, mounting location, and geometry. Some dimensions were changed in approximately 1 mm increments so physical behavior could be compared quickly.

05

Wiring, Soldering & Integration

Once individual actuators worked, the challenge became integrating them into a moving carriage. Mechanical joints, solenoids, wires, electronics, rails, and structural parts all occupied the same limited space.

I worked on actuator soldering, wire routing, harness organization, mechanical clearances, and repeated integration testing. Wiring had to remain clear of moving fingers and rail hardware while still allowing the carriage to travel along the piano.

Integration became a major part of the project

A working solenoid by itself was not enough. The actuator, linkage, wiring, carriage, electronics, and positioning system all had to work together without interfering with one another.

06

Key Positioning Model

I wrote a Python script that used keyboard geometry, rack pitch, gear tooth count, and a calibratable hardware offset to calculate motor positions for all 54 keys.

Python
Piano Key Position Mapping
key_positioning.py 
import math

PITCH = 2.0
TEETH = 39

MM_REV = TEETH * PITCH

MILLIRAD_REV = 2.0 * math.pi * 1000.0

TICKS_PER_MM = MILLIRAD_REV / MM_REV

OFFSET_TICKS = 0

NATURAL = 22.25
OCTAVE = 7 * NATURAL

CENTER = {
    'C': 0.5,
    'C#': 0.95,
    'D': 1.5,
    'D#': 2.05,
    'E': 2.5,
    'F': 3.5,
    'F#': 3.93,
    'G': 4.5,
    'G#': 5.0,
    'A': 5.5,
    'A#': 6.07,
    'B': 6.5
}

NOTES = [
    'C', 'C#', 'D', 'D#',
    'E', 'F', 'F#', 'G',
    'G#', 'A', 'A#', 'B'
]

NUM_KEYS = 54

keys = []

for i in range(NUM_KEYS):

    oct_idx = i // 12

    note = NOTES[i % 12]

    mm = (
        oct_idx * OCTAVE
        + CENTER[note] * NATURAL
    )

    keys.append(mm)

for i, mm in enumerate(keys):

    ticks = round(
        mm * TICKS_PER_MM
        + OFFSET_TICKS
    )

    comma = "," if i < NUM_KEYS - 1 else ""

    print(
        f"    {ticks:<8}{comma} [{i}]"
    )
